SciVerify · feature

Fluent isn't the same as written by a person.

SciVerify scans every IMRaD section with 22 indicators across six buckets — pasted chatbot prefixes, tortured phrasing, hallucinated citations, cadence anomalies, plus a local GPT-2 perplexity and burstiness model — and highlights the exact words that triggered each flag.

What it is

A transparent, heuristics-plus-models screen — not a black-box classifier. Twenty-two indicators are organized into six buckets (model, lexical, cadence, semantic, citations, cross-section) and applied per section. A calibrated logistic-regression model turns the buckets into one AI probability, while a single chatbot leak or tortured phrase still forces the section into the fail band on its own.

What it can do

  • 22 indicators, six buckets. Critical signals like chatbot leaks and tortured paraphrase sit beside statistical ones like sentence rhythm, discourse-marker openings, and low burstiness.
  • Local model signals. A small GPT-2 model scores each sentence's perplexity and burstiness — machine text is uniformly predictable, human prose varies.
  • ZipPy compression entropy. Text that compresses suspiciously well is another tell of machine generation.
  • Calibrated verdicts. A trained logistic-regression model combines the buckets into one 0–100 AI probability per section.
  • Cloud LLM second opinion. An optional detector cross-checks the heuristics and proposes concrete fixes per passage.
  • Per-section scoring. Introduction, methods, results, discussion and conclusion are scored separately, with the trigger words highlighted.
  • Banded verdicts. Sections fall into likely-AI, mixed, or likely-human bands; critical hits force ≥ 90.
  • Cached & re-runnable. Results invalidate automatically when the main article is re-uploaded.
